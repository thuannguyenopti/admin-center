# Editorial and Visual-Subject Surfaces

Use this authority for DAM, Brand Packs, Opal creative-system UI, typography, color, imagery, logos, templates and other subjects whose quality must be seen before it can be edited.

“Editorial” means the subject receives enough scale, whitespace and composition to be judged. It does not mean decorative, magazine-like or exempt from Axiom.

## Editoriality dial

Raise editoriality when users evaluate creative quality, compare visual assets, browse outputs or understand a brand system.

Lower editoriality when users edit permissions, mappings, rules, technical configuration or dense operational data. Use the standard configuration form model for those tasks.

Creative-system data should feel inspectable and curated before it feels editable:

- typography as a type specimen;
- imagery as a visual library;
- color as a brand system;
- logos as inspectable marks;
- templates as output examples.

Controls support the subject and must not dominate it.

## Surface patterns

Use:

- a large specimen or preview for the selected visual subject;
- grid browsing when users recognize peers visually;
- focused sheets/modals for editing one visual asset;
- category navigation only when it improves browsing;
- metadata grouped quietly below or beside the preview;
- one high-value add/create/upload action for the current scope;
- real, intentional aspect ratios and crops.

Do not:

- lead with form inputs before the subject is understood;
- repeat a form card for every asset;
- shrink previews to make metadata fit;
- treat creative assets as generic admin rows;
- use random photography as a logo placeholder;
- use broken, duplicate or generic placeholder imagery when realistic content exists.

## Typography specimens

A useful specimen includes:

- family name;
- a large `Aa`, wordmark or meaningful brand phrase;
- a pangram or realistic brand sentence;
- uppercase, lowercase and numerals;
- available weights/styles;
- source/status: library, uploaded, linked or name-only metadata.

Do not add decorative captions, artwork or samples that do not improve evaluation. Editing controls remain subordinate to the specimen.

## Logo specimens

Show:

- dark-on-light and light-on-dark variants when available;
- enough scale to inspect edge quality, clear space and proportions;
- the distinction between the logo file, its preview and supporting photography;
- neutral specimen surfaces unless the brand system requires otherwise.

Do not float tiny marks in large empty cards or substitute unrelated photography.

## Color specimens

Show:

- sufficiently large swatches;
- accessible labels;
- role and expected usage, not only a hex value;
- pairing/contrast context when useful;
- quiet, subordinate editing controls.

Color-system surfaces should explain relationships, not resemble a spreadsheet of values.

## Imagery and templates

Show:

- real product, campaign or action context;
- consistent, intentional aspect ratios within peer groups;
- full-size focus when the user must judge crop or quality;
- realistic library names;
- tags only when they support filtering or recognition.

Template previews should feel like real brand outputs, not marketplace filler or faux screenshots.

## Asset comparison

Current and pending assets must represent the same underlying subject and use compatible crop/scale. If the media cannot be compared visually, do not present it as a visual diff.

Choose the mechanism that best reveals change:

- side-by-side for peer inspection;
- overlay/scrubber for aligned pixels;
- rapid toggle for same-frame alternatives;
- annotated single view when only one local region changed.

Keep the changed evidence more prominent than scores, AI personality or metadata.

## Brand Packs and Stride prototype content

Brand Packs should feel like brand intelligence, not a template marketplace. For prototype work, `Stride` is the primary fictive endurance sports/triathlon brand.

Stride content should feel endurance-driven, high-performance, athletic, professional, editorial but real, and focused on swimming, cycling, running, transitions and race day.

Prefer real athletes in action:

- open-water or competitive pool swimmers;
- professional road cyclists, pelotons, time trials and climbs;
- race, track and road-running packs;
- active transition or race preparation;
- motion, effort, pace, water, road, weather and finish-line context.

Avoid merely adjacent fitness imagery:

- isolated bikes or generic objects;
- yoga, stretching, gym classes, dumbbells or wellness lifestyle;
- posed fitness portraits or influencer workouts;
- casual jogging without race/training context;
- broken, duplicate or generic placeholder images.

For Stride logos, use intentional light/dark wordmark specimens rather than photography. For templates, use action sport creative, race-day energy and plausible wordmark placement. Prefer fewer credible examples over many weak ones.

Use realistic names such as `Open Water Race Pack`, `Peloton Race Attack`, `Finish Chute Sprint` or `Aero Position Push`, not `Image 1`, `Bike photo` or `Hero`.

## Acceptance check

The subject must be understandable before editing begins, large enough to judge, supported by quiet metadata and presented with the interaction model appropriate to its evaluation. High editoriality still must pass the Axiom and visual-quality gates.
